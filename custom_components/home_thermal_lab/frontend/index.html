Dashed lines after the "now" mark are the forecast for the next day (the usual day plus what the zone model, with the thermostat and the neighbouring zones simulated, says differs today). The dotted line is what the forecast said three hours earlier, laid over what happened. The faint lines are the other rooms on the same thermostat. The strip has three lanes. Heating (red) and cooling (teal) show this zone's calls in the upper half of each lane and the calls of the neighbouring zones that bleed into it in the lower, lighter half. The fan lane (grey) is this zone's fan running while no other zone is calling for heat or cool; each zone makes its own call, so the fan alone differs from zone to zone. After the "now" mark, each lane's upper half shows the forecast for this zone: the furnace duty (heating), the air conditioner (simulated from the forecast weather once it has learned its stop and start temperatures, otherwise the usual day) and the fan alone (the usual day, house-wide). The room forecast responds to all three, including the other zones' cooling and the fan pulling heat from every room. The room chart keeps one scale for every zone, and shows the outdoor temperature only where it comes into that range; the outdoor chart below keeps one scale all year.

Rooms: calculated against fitted

Heat loss and cooling are Manual J at the design conditions, from House Structure's areas and the nominal R-values (the house defaults). UA calc is the room's calculated conductance (walls, roofs, windows and infiltration). UA fitted is the zone model's measured cooling rate turned into the same units, so a ratio near 1 means the nominal envelope matches the house. The envelope scale is the factor on the nominal conductances that would match the fit: above 1 means more heat flows than the nominal R-values give. Walls below grade are left out of the fit, which has its own ground term.
