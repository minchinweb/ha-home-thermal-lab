# Other findings

Things learned about the house along the way that are worth keeping, even when they do not help the model much.

## When the kitchen's east window gets morning sun (2 November 2026)

The small east kitchen window (12 x 60 in) is in a 4.6 ft stub of east wall at the north end of the kitchen wing. The main block's north gable starts about 8 in south of the glass and runs 25 ft east, so the window only gets direct sun while the sun is north of about 102 degrees (the wall faces 100.4). From late September to mid-March the sun rises too far south and the house hides it completely.

Clear-sky sun on the glass, from `scripts/house_shading.py` (the OpenStreetMap outline, the 12:12 roof, and the window's measured position), taking trees and houses across the street as blocking below 6 degrees, and 70% of the light through the glass:

| Date | Sun on the glass (local time) | Heat through the glass |
|---|---|---|
| 21 March | about 08:25 to 08:45 (20 min) | about 40 Wh |
| 15 April | 07:25 to 09:10 | about 270 Wh |
| 15 May | 06:30 to 09:40 | about 510 Wh |
| 21 June | 06:05 to 10:00 | about 610 Wh |
| 1 August | 06:45 to 09:45 | about 490 Wh |
| 1 September | 07:35 to 09:05 | about 225 Wh |
| 21 September | about 08:10 to 08:30 (20 min) | about 40 Wh |
| late September to mid-March | none | 0 |

At least 30 minutes a day of sun from 22 March to 20 September, so roughly equinox to equinox. The peak is about 200 W through the glass, in the early morning. Even at midsummer the window adds at most about 0.6 kWh a day, roughly 0.3 C on the kitchen if nothing else moved it. The street trees in leaf (May to September) may take some of the earliest sun. A photo out the window on a sunny morning around 08:00 in April would confirm it.

## The patio door and the main block

The main block reaches 13 ft further south than the kitchen wing, just east of the patio door, so it hides low sun from the door when the sun is east of about 150 degrees, and part of it up to about 172 degrees. In winter the patio door's sun comes only around solar noon and after. On 2 November it began at about 12:15 (sun at 166 degrees, three quarters of the door lit). See hypothesis 11 for the details.
